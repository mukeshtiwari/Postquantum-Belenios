\section{Vision}
Recent growth in quantum technology is threating all kind of classical public-key cryptography but one place where it is very 
threatining the most is electronic ballot privacy. Many countries conduct online election for the citizens living abroad, e.g., France,
Switzerland, Estonia, etc. To ensure universal verifiability --any one can verify the election outcome-- encrypted ballots and their 
corresponding validity in terms of zero-knowloedge proof is published. This however threatns the privacy because all the encryption 
and zero-knowledge proof is based on the discrete-logarithm assumption that can be easily broken by a quantum computer. Therefore, we propose an 
alterantive: migrating all the primitives in Belenios to post-quantum setting. Belenios is one of the most widely used and scrutinised 
voting system. In addition, it supports simple approval and list votes through homomorphic tallying, and complex methods (Condorcet–Schulze, 
Single Transferable Vote and Majority Judgment) through verifiable mix-nets.

\section{From classical to post-quantum primitives in Belenios}
Given that Belenios~\cite{cortier2019belenios} is an old and mature system that supports many classical
primitives such as ElGamal encryption, Schnorr signatures, Chaum--Pedersen and disjunctive zero-knowledge proofs,
Pedersen threshold key generation, and the Terelius--Wikstr\"om proof of shuffle, it is not possible to 
migrate all these primitives in 12 months to a post-quantum setting. In addition, for some of these classical 
primitives, there are no corresponding quantum primitives. Therefore, our goal with project is to:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Standardised primitives with existing machine-checked proofs.} Ballot signatures move to ML-DSA 
and trustee channels to ML-KEM. Both are NIST standards whose security proofs have already been machine-checked 
in EasyCrypt~\cite{barbosa2023dilithium,almeida2024mlkem}. ML-DSA generates its keys deterministically from a 
32-byte seed, so Belenios can keep deriving a voter's signing key from their private credential. Here the 
work is integration rather than new proof.
\item \textbf{Lattice protocols for the mix-net: the core of this project.} BGV encryption, the lattice 
shuffle proof and verifiable distributed decryption~\cite{aranha2023mixnet} together give a post-quantum 
version of every step of a Belenios mix-net election, from casting a ballot to publishing the result. None 
of them has a machine-checked proof. Each has a classical counterpart that does (ElGamal and the 
Terelius--Wikstr\"om shuffle~\cite{cortier2018belenioseasycrypt,haines2019verified,287095}), which makes 
them a good test of whether AI can carry an existing proof across to the post-quantum setting. Distributed 
decryption is the hardest of the three, because its noise-drowning argument is statistical; we expect it to 
show where current AI falls short.
\end{itemize}


By the end of the project, a Schulze, STV or Majority Judgment election in Belenios can run on post-quantum 
primitives from start to finish, apart from key generation, with its core cryptographic components machine-checked.
